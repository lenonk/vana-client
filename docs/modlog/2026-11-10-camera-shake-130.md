## Camera shake: element order, the busy test, retail frames; the pad rumbles (2026-11-10)

Modlog-gaps item 130 ("Camera shake: retail's element order within a frame; FUN_10087070(0)'s bits; which consumers
read the shaken matrix; the rumble sources and pad flags; above 30 fps retail shakes faster (n = 1); lobby / creator /
title cameras do not shake"), the open points of "Camera shake and rumble: element update op 0x5f". Traced in
Steven's FFXiMain.dll (30261006_0; disassembly and Ghidra).

- Element order, fixed. Every element is a task, and the factory FUN_1004e8d0 ends every kind the same way
  (0x1005119c..0x100511ba): `if FUN_10054410() != 0: FUN_100749b0(elem, FUN_10054410())`. FUN_10054410 returns
  DAT_10353710, 0xb in the image (its setter 0x10054400 has no caller), and FUN_100749b0 -> FUN_10074f80 unlinks the
  task and puts it right after bucket 0xb's sentinel. So the walk (FUN_10074c30, head to tail) runs the elements
  newest first, after every generator, in the frame they are made. Elements made in one frame come in the reverse
  of their generators' order: a generator's proxy task ('Mg', vtable 0x1032b654, +0x24 = FUN_10054220 ->
  FUN_100496f0(0)) is placed by FUN_100531d0 from its attach kind ((+0x36 & 1) << 4 | +0x34 & 0xf): 0, 0xe, 0xf and
  0x14 through FUN_10074a00 -> FUN_10075120 (just before the running task, or at the end of bucket 8 when none
  runs: start order), the actor-attached kinds at the front of bucket 9 (the last started runs first).
  Ours ran op 0x5f in element slot order. FUN_10021ba0 only raises the speed when a kick outruns twice the speed, so
  two elements' kicks leave a different speed by their order (0.03 then 0.01 ends at 0.01 / 8, the other way at
  0.03 / 8). Now `Effects::run_order` (the tick an element was made in, its generator's place, its serial), newest
  first. *(inf: an unattached generator started by a running task goes just before that task.)*
- The busy test, fixed. FUN_100adf70 = !FUN_10087070(0) && bit 17: FUN_10087070(0) is the busy test of "Command
  gate: busy states" (an event, FreezeFlag +0x128 bit 15, CharmFlag +0x12c bit 13, the fishing / digging statuses
  of FUN_100956c0). Ours only knew events, so "Screen shaking" off also took the rod's `vib1` shake and rumble away
  while fishing (and the shake while frozen or charmed). `Effects::busy` = textcmd::busy.
- Frames, fixed. FUN_10021900's n = ftol(min(FUN_10014cf0(), 8)); FUN_10014cf0 = max(1, DAT_104568fc +0x28), and
  +0x28 is the 4-frame average truncated to whole ticks, at least the divisor +0x30 and at most 20 (FUN_10011a00).
  The divisor is 2; only FUN_100883e0 (the PS2-era fishing statuses LSB never sends) sets 4. So n is whole and at
  least 2: n = 1 needs a patched divisor, not a faster PC. Below 30 fps retail kicks once and steps the frame's 3
  to 8 ticks; ours repeated the kicks for every 2 ticks. `camera::retail_frame`: one retail frame of the whole ticks
  gathered once 2 are there *(not the 4-frame average)*. Frame order: the task walk (kicks), the pad poll
  FUN_10005a40 (rumble out), the limiter, then FUN_10015510 (integrator, view).
- The shaken matrix, as built. FUN_10015510 stores cam+4 as the device VIEW (FUN_1000bd50(2)) and through
  FUN_100150d0 -> FUN_10015120 in every view form the client keeps (DAT_104568fc +0x94, +0xd4, +0x114, +0x294).
  The raw eye +0x44 / look +0x50 are read by the camera control (FUN_1001ee60, FUN_10021280), effect attach
  (FUN_1003ba20), the scheduler camera (FUN_10055780), the event ops near 0x100bba11 and op 0x5f. The shake nods the
  direction about the level axis across the look, so view x (the sound pan) and the level heading (compass, ground
  cursor) are the same either way. Ours: `App::camera` (shaken) for drawing and picking, the raw camera for the rest.
- One camera class. FUN_1001e470 allocates a new camera (vtable 0x1032a37c), resets it (FUN_1001e590 ->
  FUN_1001e4f0) and makes it current; its callers include the creator (0x10245bef). FUN_10015510 steps whatever is
  current, so the lobby, creator and title cameras would shake too; no op 0x5f runs there. Nothing to build.
- Rumble, built. The pad (DAT_10455c20) sums +0xdb4 and FUN_10007ae0 reads it once a frame: L = 2 x max(sum, 0),
  under 10 none, at most 256; the three fields cleared; while the hold +0xdb8 is above 0 it loses the frame's ticks
  and, if still above 0, the frame's level is dropped; else +0xdb8 = 5; m = L x 10000 / 256. XInput (+0xc9a, with
  +0xc99 and DAT_10455c39): XInputSetState with both motors min(4 m, 0xffff) (FUN_10008ed0). DirectInput
  (DAT_10455c28): a constant force of m on one axis, sqrt(2 m^2) with direction (m, m) on two (DIEFFECT flags 0x12,
  SetParameters 0x20000140), skipped when it and the last (+0xdc0) are 0.
  Flags: padmode000 fills DAT_10455c34 pad on, c39 force feedback, c36 sliders, c35 hats, c37 background, c38 XInput
  in that order (FUN_10005490); +0xc9a = the XInput library is up (FUN_10008d90, only with c38), +0xc99 = the chosen
  device is an XInput pad (FUN_10008ab0 on its GUID), +0xc9c its user index.
  Sources: (a) op 0x5f's v; (b) the chat call task, 60 a frame while bit 5 of its whole timer is set and its flag
  holds; (c) the creator dancer's foley (FUN_10252ce0: event type 1 sets a level added every frame, type 2 adds 1
  while its value is above 0x7f). All eight races' trigger files (31728..31735) hold type 0 only, so (c) never fires.
  Ours: `gamepad::Rumble` (FUN_10007ae0's XInput branch, tested against the hold), one constant gilrs effect on the
  pad that plays with the level as its gain, off when the pad is off or the game is in the background without the
  background option. Config `gamepad_rumble` (default on; retail's padmode000 [1] turns it off as [0] does the pad),
  the Controller window's "Enable Force Feedback". Checked on an Xbox Series pad on Linux (the effect is taken);
  not felt by a person yet.
- The chat call task, built. FUN_100380f0 keeps one task (DAT_10463938): a call that comes while it lives plays
  nothing. Timer +0x34 = 120 ticks down by the frame's ticks, the voice at +0x38, flag +0x3c. FUN_100381d0: once the
  timer is under 0 and the voice is over (FUN_100367f0), or while DAT_10351710 is 0, it ends; else the rumble above
  (timer 120..96, 63..32, then -1..-32 and on while the sound lasts). The flag (0x10127c8a): 1 for `<call>`;
  `<ncall>` (table value | 0x800000) FUN_10127ab0, true when the line's "(name)" is us or an actor of that name
  within 4 on whole x / z; `<scall>` (| 0x400000) 0. The "blink" of "Chat call sounds" was this rumble. Ours:
  `CallTask`, the call sound under its voice handle.
- The tell tone, fixed. FUN_10127990's 0x30703430 for a received tell carries the 0x400000 flag, taken off before
  the lookup (0x10127cb6): SeSep "0040" (DAT 0, se 40, sound/win/se/se000/se000040.spw), not "0p40". Retail plays
  that short tone through the call task, with no rumble. Ours had played the `<call>` sound 0017 as an interface
  sound; now 40 through the call path (config 0x47, the call level, one at a time).
- Open: equal draw keys. The ordering table (FUN_100553c0) keeps equal clamped keys in insertion order, and the
  elements insert in their update (vtable 0x1032b018 +0x20 FUN_10045a40 -> FUN_100458f0), i.e. newest element
  first. `Effects::insert_order` ("Effect draw order", Port Bastok's harbour) sorts by the generator's start and
  the element's serial, oldest first, from the insert-before-running path (FUN_10075120, 2019's FUN_10074170)
  that only generator proxies take. Not changed here: the harbour's three generators need their start order and
  a retail picture first. Also open: ours runs op 0x5f before the frame's emissions, so a new element's first kick
  comes a frame after retail's.
